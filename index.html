Band-Edge-Extended Heterointerfaces for perovskite Solar Cells and Tandems
👤 Bitao Dong, Meijie Cui, Cong Chen et al. 📅 2026-11-18 📰 Nature
Nature perovskite
📝 原文摘要
Perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have emerged as a promising photovoltaic technology, with efficiencies rapidly approaching those of established crystalline-silicon solar cells. Further efficiency gains, however, are increasingly limited by charge extraction and recombination at the interface between the perovskite absorber and the electron-transport layer. Interfacial modifiers are widely used to suppress these losses, yet many conventional ammonium-based modifiers are weakly electronically coupled to the perovskite surface and can introduce barriers to electron extraction 1-5. Here we realize electronically coupled heterointerfaces by introducing a family of heteroatom-containing, fully conjugated formamidinium interfacial modulators, establishing band-edge-extending conjugated heterointerfaces that overcome the bottleneck of inefficient interfacial electron transfer. The resulting band-edge-extended heterointerface establishes delocalized electron connectivity at the perovskite/electron transporting layer (ETL) interface and suppresses interfacial non-radiative recombination. We report a certified power-conversion efficiency (PCE) of 27.96% and a stabilized power output (SPO) of 27.84% for inverted PSCs. The corresponding devices retained over 95% of their initial efficiency after 2000 h continuous maximum-power-point tracking under 1-sun irradiation (ISOS-L). Extending this strategy to monolithic perovskite–silicon tandems enables a certified SPO of 34.71%, establishing band-edge extension as a broadly applicable approach to overcoming the conventional trade-off between interfacial passivation and charge extraction.

Fully integrated perovskite active polaritonic circuits
👤 Antonio Fieramosca, Vincenzo Ardizzone, Rosanna Mastria et al. 📅 2026-11-18 📰 Nature
Nature perovskite
📝 原文摘要
Photonic integrated circuits are emerging as a key technology for compact, analogue and energy-efficient optical information processing1. Yet, their practical implementation remains limited by the intrinsically weak optical nonlinearities of conventional materials, which demand high power and large device footprints to achieve substantial nonlinear responses2. Exciton–polaritons, hybrid light–matter excitations of semiconducting materials, offer a promising route to overcome these limitations by combining strong optical nonlinearities with the high speed and scalability typical of photonic platforms3,4,5. However, despite their potential, fully integrated on-chip polaritonic systems simultaneously delivering room-temperature waveguide lasing, controllable optical routing and amplification have remained elusive. Here we demonstrate a fully integrated perovskite polaritonic circuit that brings these functionalities together in a single-step-grown platform. We realize precise and scalable polariton circuits with integrated gratings that act as both couplers and reflectors, enabling in-plane Fabry–Pérot cavities. These structures support robust in-plane polariton lasing with coherent guided emission at a geometry-defined wavelength, optical amplification of propagating polaritons and nonlinear optical phase control exceeding π, all within the same on-chip architecture. Our results establish perovskite polariton circuits as a scalable platform for active and interferometric integrated photonics.

👤 W. Sandholt, N. Gauquelin, J. Mangeri et al. 📅 2026-11-18 📰 Nature
Nature perovskite
📝 原文摘要
Twisting two atomic layers produces a geometric moiré pattern but bonding-induced interfacial reconstruction fundamentally transforms this into an ordered dislocation network—a distinction obscured in weakly bonded van der Waals systems. Although in-plane topological vortex nanostructures arising from twisting-induced lateral strain modulation have been linked to periodic moiré patterns in freestanding perovskite layers1,2,3 and 2D bilayers4, their coupling to the interfacial dislocation network in twisted layers remains unresolved. Here we demonstrate that twisted freestanding SrTiO3 (STO) layers undergo interfacial reconstruction into a network of screw dislocations, accompanied by the emergence of in-plane topological vortices. Unlike in previous reports, these vortices are associated with the dislocation network rather than with geometric moiré patterns. Four-dimensional scanning transmission electron microscopy (4DSTEM) reveals a beam deflection texture in registry with the interfacial dislocation network. A machine learning interatomic potential, trained on first-principles calculations, together with phase-field modelling, confirms that competing strains within the dislocation network stabilize polar vortex–antivortex pairs; first-principles calculations further reveal the emergence of an electronic superlattice with a well-defined periodicity. Our results establish twist-controlled dislocation networks as a new and versatile route to designing local polar and electronic structures in oxide materials.

Octahedral connectivity as a design principle in perovskite oxides for catalytic applications
👤 Unknown 📅 2026-11-17 📰 Energy & Environmental Science
Energy & Environmental Science perovskite
📝 原文摘要
Octahedral connectivity, the dimensionality and mode by which BO6 polyhedra are connected across a perovskite lattice, is a dynamic, tunable, and structural descriptor. The electronic structure, defect formation, charge transport anisotropy, surface termination chemistry, and, ultimately, catalytic function of perovskite oxides are significantly influenced by their octahedral connectivity. While simple perovskites feature corner-sharing BO6 networks in three dimensions, their low-dimensional analogues, spanning two-dimensional (2D), one-dimensional (1D), and zero-dimensional (0D) frameworks, with corner-sharing, edge-sharing, face-sharing, or isolated octahedra, offer new opportunities to tailor structure–property relationships. Reducing connectivity from 3D to 0D systematically decreases oxygen vacancy formation energy, introduces structural anisotropy and ordered vacancy channels, and produces B-site coordination environments that allow catalytic functions inaccessible in the fully connected 3D parent structure. This review systematically examines the diversity of octahedral connectivity across perovskite oxide-based materials, containing different connection modes. We analyze how these structural motifs influence defect chemistry and interfacial behavior, with a focus on oxygen vacancies, cation non-stoichiometry, and octahedral connectivity.
