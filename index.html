📝 原文摘要
Twisting and chiral growth are unusual in crystals and rarely observed in layered materials. Here we show that layered achiral metal-halide perovskites spontaneously form twisted and helical ribbons. Microscopy, diffraction and molecular dynamics simulations reveal that hydrogen-bonded organic networks within the lattice generate mismatched strains, causing ribbons to bend and twist when the strain is released, similar to the chiral opening of seed pods. This mismatch, known as geometric frustration, originates from crystal symmetry and can be tuned through molecular doping. Controlling growth kinetics twists nearly all ribbons towards one direction, giving bulk samples chiroptical activity. Statistical and mechanical analyses show that ribbon shape is governed by intrinsic elastic strain and geometric constraints; modulating them enables a continuous transition from twisted to helical ribbons. Our results show how molecular-scale interactions can generate macroscopic chiral shapes, providing a route to control chiral morphologies in layered two-dimensional materials.
