An in situ redox-converted molecule for all-perovskite tandem solar cells
👤 Shaomin Yang, Chunxiang Lan, Miaomiao Zeng et al. 📅 2026-11-25 📰 Nature
Nature perovskite
📝 原文摘要
All-perovskite tandem solar cells (TSCs) are gaining prominence due to their ability to surpass the efficiency limit of single-junction perovskite solar cells (PSCs)1,2. However, the efficiency and stability of all-perovskite TSCs are limited by chemical instability at the hole transport layer/perovskite interface in the tin-lead (Sn-Pb) narrow-bandgap subcell, which introduces deep trap states from tin cations and promotes non-radiative recombination3-5. Here we incorporate a redox-converted molecule of p-aminobenzohydrazide (ABH) into poly(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene):polystyrene sulfonate (PEDOT:PSS) that neutralizes PSS acidity, heals Sn4+ defects, and generates a dipole passivation effect upon oxidation. During the preparation of Sn-Pb perovskites, ABH suppresses interfacial oxidation by reducing Sn4+ defects and converts to p-aminobenzaldehyde (ABD), which suppresses photogenerated hole-induced Sn2+ oxidation, aligning energy levels for efficient hole extraction. The in situ redox-converted molecule stabilizes the buried interface during both device fabrication and operation, reduces non-radiative recombination and enhances stability. The resulting narrow-bandgap Sn-Pb PSC achieves a power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 25.4% (certified 25.2%) with an open-circuit voltage of 0.92 V and a fill factor of 83.4%. Integrated into monolithic all-perovskite TSCs, the device delivers a PCE of 31.1% (certified 30.8%) together with enhanced thermal (ISOS-D-2I, 85 °C) and operational stability (ISOS-L-1I) under inert conditions.
