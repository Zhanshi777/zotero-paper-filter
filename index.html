Rigidity-driven molecular assembly for high-efficiency flexible perovskite/CIGS tandems
👤 Libing Yao, Enbing Bi, Qingqing Liu et al. 📅 2026-11-27 📰 Nature Communications
Nature Communications perovskite
📝 原文摘要
Monolithic flexible perovskite/Cu(In,Ga)Se2 (CIGS) tandem solar cells (TSCs) are pivotal for next-generation flexible photovoltaics, owing to their high power-to-weight ratio and mechanical compliance. However, their development is constrained by the challenge of depositing high-quality hole-selective contacts (HSCs) onto intrinsically rough CIGS bottom cells. Conventional molecular interlayers often exhibit poor packing on such surfaces, leading to interfacial defects that compromise both efficiency and stability. Here, we report Py6, a rigid molecule engineered with an enforced vinylene (C = C) bridge to ensure molecular planarity and extended π-conjugation. This structural rigidity fosters a highly ordered nanoscale assembly that effectively templates the growth of dense, void-free perovskite layers while optimizing interfacial energetic alignment. Consequently, our champion flexible TSC achieved a power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 28.21% (0.0749 cm2, certified 27.58%), while large-area devices (1.010 cm2) reached 25.63%, with a certified PCE of 25.19% and a certified stabilized PCE of 25.16%. Furthermore, the resulting devices exhibit exceptional durability, retaining 93.8% of their initial PCE after 400 hours of continuous 1-sun operation and 94.2% after 5000 rigorous bending cycles. Our rigidity-driven molecular design overcomes the critical interfacial bottlenecks of non-planar photovoltaics, providing a robust pathway for high-performance, mechanically resilient flexible tandem technologies.

Polymerizable ionic buffers transform grain boundaries into ion-regulating interfaces in metal halide perovskites
👤 Unknown 📅 2026-11-26 📰 Energy & Environmental Science
Energy & Environmental Science perovskite
📝 原文摘要
Long-term operational instability remains a critical barrier for perovskite photovoltaics toward commercial deployment, originating largely from uncontrolled ionic migration across grain boundaries. Mobile A-site cations and halide ions transform grain boundaries in metal-halide perovskites into nanoscale ion-transport pathways, undermining device stability. Here, we develop a polymerizable imidazolium tetrafluoroborate ionic buffer that chemically stabilizes mobile A- and X-site species while physically confining grain-boundary pathways. The BF4− counterion interacts with undercoordinated Pb sites, whereas the propargyl-substituted imidazolium cation stabilizes organic A-site cations, suppresses deprotonation, and scavenges reactive iodine species. Upon post-deposition activation, the cation undergoes in situ polymerization within grain-boundary regions to form an amorphous intergranular network. Correlative nanoscale characterization reveals reduced ion redistribution, electrostatic heterogeneity, and local electrical hysteresis. The inverted perovskite solar cells reach 27.25% efficiency (certified 27.03%), while 13.95 cm2 modules achieve a certified 24.10% efficiency and retain 95% of their initial performance after 2000 h of continuous maximum power point tracking. Outdoor testing supports the practical viability of this grain-boundary protection strategy.
