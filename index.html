📝 原文摘要
Halide perovskite synthesis generally uses toxic, high-boiling-point and strongly coordinating organic solvents, hindering scalable processing and crystallographic control in thick-film applications. Water is ideal as a solvent but incompatible with perovskite synthesis due to precursor insolubility and rapid hydrolysis in water. Here we introduce a hydrogel-confined reaction environment that overcomes this incompatibility by decoupling precursor solvation from lattice formation. Within a polymer–water network, PbI2 and organic iodides are dispersed in a stabilized ionic state that suppresses aggregation and kinetically inhibits water-induced decomposition. Concurrently, this network anchors emerging nuclei and imposes spatial confinement, directing crystal growth into dense thick films. Spectroscopic and computational analysis reveals facet-specific coordination between polymer and perovskite, suppressing surface defect formation and preserving efficient charge transport despite the presence of the polymer matrix. The resultant thick hydrogel-grown films (180 µm) exhibit charge carrier mobilities of ∼10 cm2 V−1 s−1.
